
## Why I stopped paying for courses and started trusting these high-quality open-source repositories

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*ZaCO9w_w4a2QiE1gEK4fMw.png)

If you are reading this, chances are you have already fallen into what is called the “**_Tutorial Hell Trap_**”.

You have binged countless YouTube tutorials, completed a number of Udemy courses, and probably earned a stack of digital certificates. You feel like you are learning, but when faced with a blank VS Code window or real-world coding challenge, your mind goes blank.

It is a common problem where passive consumption masquerades active learning.

You are watching code, not **_writing_** it.  
You are observing solutions, not **_building_** them on your own.

With the pace at which tech is evolving in 2026, this passive approach isn’t going to help you much. But luckily, there is a way out! A set of resources that forces you to **_think_**, **_build_**, and **_truly understand_**?

In this blog, I am sharing resources that will be your survival guide in escaping the tutorial trap and levelling up your engineering skills.

If all this sounds promising to you, let us go ahead and break the cycle!

### [Build Your Own X](https://github.com/codecrafters-io/build-your-own-x)

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/0*1hKEKxOMt9wvywxq)

We, the software engineers, make use of a number of technologies like Docker, Git, SQLite, and Reddis. Most of the times, we take these technologies for granted without even understanding the level of engineering that has gone into building those complex systems.

Most tutorials only scratch the surface, teaching you how to use these tools. But to move from a user to a genuine engineer, you need to understand the core mechanism of these popular technologies. This is exactly where our very first repo `[codecrafters-io/build-your-own-x](https://github.com/codecrafters-io/build-your-own-x)` enters the scene.

This extraordinary repository doesn’t just explain complex tools. It provides step-by-step guide to help you build your own versions of popular softwares. Imagine building your own Git client, your own Redis server, or even your own Docker.

This is where true understanding is forged. It challenges you to grapple with data structures, network protocols, and low-level logic.

If you can build a version of a real-world tool, you are not just watching, you are becoming an architect!

### [Building Real-World Applications](https://github.com/gothinkster/realworld)

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1308/0*pZ8T9YIY4wlQQFTv.gif)

One of the biggest hurdles for developers stuck in tutorial hell is the inability to transition from small, isolated examples to complete, robust applications. Your portfolio is likely filled with basic projects that don’t reflect real-world complexity.

Welcome `[gothinkster/realworld](https://github.com/gothinkster/realworld)`.

Dubbed as “**_The Mother of All Demo Apps_**”, this repository provides a full-stack Medium.com clone specification. It is implemented in virtually every popular frontend (FE) and backend (BE) framework you can imagine.

This isn’t a tutorial with a solution. You get a clear API spec, UI mockups, and user stories. Your task is to build it yourself in your chosen stack. It forces you to think about authentication, data flow, error handling, and deployment!

Building a “Conduit” app will give you a project for your portfolio that truly demonstrate your capabilities.

### [Sharpening Your JavaScript Mind](https://github.com/lydiahallie/javascript-questions)

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1200/0*yaboHp5sHNX1cHVD.png)

JavaScript is the unchallenged king of frontends. From startups to big tech, all use JavaScript to develop frontend and thus it is utmost important to learn & master this language.

Tutorials often gloss over the deeper, more challenging aspects of the language. If you want to impress interviewers and build robust applications, your understanding needs to go beyond the surface.

My previous blog highlighted `[javascript-questions](https://github.com/lydiahallie/javascript-questions)` and `[you-dont-know-js](https://github.com/getify/You-Dont-Know-JS)`. They remain absolutely essential.

### [System Design Guide](https://github.com/ByteByteGoHq/system-design-101)

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/0*x2c_YHwWb7zNFj_Q.jpg)

If your skill set is limited to just writing code, unfortunately, you won’t be able to climb up the ladder to becoming a senior developer. To lead, innovate, and solve real business problems, you need to think at a system level. This is often the biggest leap out of the tutorial hell.

The `[what-happens-when](https://github.com/alex/what-happens-when)` repo is an excellent starting point. It takes classic interview questions like “What happens when you type google.com and press enter?” and breaks them down to astonishing details. Understanding this single flow illuminates networking, DNS, load balancing, caching, and more. It trains your mind to see beyond codebase.

Building on that, `[system-design-101](https://github.com/ByteByteGoHq/system-design-101)` and `[system-design-resources](https://github.com/ashishps1/awesome-system-design-resources)` provide the comprehensive knowledge you need. These aren’t just theoretical, they force you to think about scalability, reliability, security, and trade-offs.

### [Project Generator](https://github.com/florinpop17/app-ideas)

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/0*Ejtzy7KyCarIzcAR)

Here is the cure for “What should I build next?”.

One of the biggest blockers to escaping tutorial hell is the inability to come up with original project ideas. `florinpop17/app-ideas` is your answer.

This repository is a goldmine of ideas, categorized by difficulty, complete with user stories and suggested features. The crucial difference is that it provides no solutions. It gives you the problem statement, and you are forces to design and implement the solution entirely on your own.

This repo forces independent thought, problem decomposition, and creative solution-finding. It is the perfect resource to build out a portfolio that showcases your independent building skills, not just your ability to follow instructions.
